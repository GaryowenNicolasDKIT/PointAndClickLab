## Overview

This Group Continuous Assessment (GCA) requires your team to design and implement a **small, highly crafted 3D playable demo in Unity**, providing approximately **2-3 minutes of focused gameplay under normal play**.

The objective is **not to build a large game**. Your team should create a small number of interconnected gameplay systems and progressively improve them throughout the semester.

The assessment emphasises **craft, technical implementation, software design, and incremental improvement**. A small, robust and polished experience is preferable to a larger game containing incomplete or poorly integrated features.

Your demo should provide opportunities to apply appropriate Unity engine features, C# programming techniques, and software design principles covered during the module.

The techniques taught during the semester form a **technical toolbox rather than a checklist**. You are expected to select and apply techniques that genuinely improve your game.

> **Using more techniques does not automatically result in a higher mark. Quality, appropriateness, integration and understanding are more important than quantity.**

The project is developed alongside the Universal Design Project (UDP). The same playable demo may be used for both modules; however, **3D Game Engine Development assesses the technical design, implementation, integration and refinement of the Unity project**.

> **Build less. Build it well. Show how you made it better.**


## How Marks Are Awarded

Meeting the stated requirements demonstrates achievement of the expected module standard but does **not automatically result in full marks**.

Approximately **70% of the available marks within each assessment criterion** recognise successful, high-quality achievement of the stated requirements.

The upper approximately **30% is reserved for H1.1-level achievement**, demonstrated through exceptional technical quality, depth, refinement, independent judgement, integration and/or problem-solving.

Additional features, greater complexity, or use of more advanced techniques do **not**, by themselves, demonstrate excellence.

> **H1.1-level excellence is primarily demonstrated by doing appropriate things exceptionally well.**

The same feature or system may be nominated as evidence for **more than one assessment criterion** where it genuinely demonstrates different aspects of achievement.

You are **not expected to create separate features for every criterion**. Depth and refinement of a small number of systems is preferable to implementing additional features simply to satisfy assessment criteria.


# Assessment Criteria


## Gameplay Systems & Integration (25 marks)

Your team should nominate **two core gameplay systems** that are central to the playable experience.

| **Element** | **Requirement** | **Score** |
|:-|:-|:-:|
| **Playable Experience** | A coherent approximately **2-3 minute playable experience** with a clear beginning, progression and conclusion. | 10 |
| **Gameplay System 1** | One nominated core gameplay system that is robust, purposeful and appropriately implemented. | 5 |
| **Gameplay System 2** | A second nominated core gameplay system that is robust, purposeful and appropriately implemented. | 5 |
| **Systems Integration** | The nominated systems interact meaningfully with the wider game and contribute to a coherent experience. | 5 |

The emphasis is on the **quality, depth and integration of a small number of systems**, not the quantity of mechanics, levels or content.


## C# & Software Design (25 marks)

Your team should nominate **three examples of its own C# code** as evidence for this criterion.

For each example, you should be able to clearly explain the:

**Problem → Solution → Benefit**

| **Element** | **Requirement** | **Score** |
|:-|:-|:-:|
| **Data & State** | One nominated example demonstrates appropriate organisation and management of game data and/or state. | 5 |
| **System Communication** | One nominated example demonstrates appropriate communication between game systems while avoiding unnecessary coupling. | 5 |
| **Software Design** | One nominated gameplay system demonstrates clear responsibilities and an appropriate design approach such as composition, interfaces and/or a suitable design pattern. | 10 |
| **Code Quality** | Nominated code is readable, consistently structured and uses appropriate C# conventions and useful documentation. | 5 |

ScriptableObjects, events, delegates, interfaces, composition and design patterns are examples of techniques that may support these requirements.

They should be used where they provide a **genuine architectural benefit**, rather than simply to satisfy a checklist.


## Technical Craft (20 marks)

Your team should nominate **two interactions or systems** that demonstrate deliberate technical refinement beyond simply making the feature functional.

| **Element** | **Requirement** | **Score** |
|:-|:-|:-:|
| **Craft Example 1** | One nominated interaction/system demonstrates purposeful refinement of its behaviour, timing, presentation and/or feedback. | 8 |
| **Craft Example 2** | A second nominated interaction/system demonstrates purposeful refinement of its behaviour, timing, presentation and/or feedback. | 8 |
| **Overall Cohesion** | Technical craft is evident across the wider demo and contributes to a consistent and coherent experience. | 4 |

Technical craft might involve appropriate use of:

- movement;
- timing;
- interpolation;
- easing;
- animation;
- camera behaviour;
- audio;
- visual effects;
- UI feedback;
- transitions;
- game-state feedback;
- orchestration between systems.

You are **not expected to use all of these techniques**.

Craft is demonstrated through **attention to detail and quality of execution**, rather than asset complexity or the number of effects used.


## Iteration & Improvement (20 marks)

Your team should nominate **two significant examples** demonstrating how an implementation was improved during development.

Each example should clearly demonstrate:

**Initial Implementation → Identified Problem → Change → Resulting Improvement**

| **Element** | **Requirement** | **Score** |
|:-|:-|:-:|
| **Improvement 1** | One significant before-and-after example demonstrating meaningful technical refinement, redesign or refactoring. | 8 |
| **Improvement 2** | A second significant before-and-after example demonstrating meaningful technical refinement, redesign or refactoring. | 8 |
| **Development History** | GitHub provides convincing evidence that the project was developed through meaningful, incremental development. | 4 |

Each nominated improvement should identify relevant **Git commits or versions** that allow the development of the feature to be examined.

Meaningful improvement may include:

- refining gameplay behaviour;
- improving architecture;
- reducing unnecessary coupling;
- improving game feel;
- improving timing or feedback;
- refactoring an implementation;
- simplifying an unnecessarily complicated solution;
- fixing edge cases or unreliable behaviour;
- improving performance;
- responding to testing or feedback.

Simply adding additional features does **not** constitute meaningful iteration.


## Technical Finish & Performance (10 marks)

# Technical Opportunities

The techniques taught during the semester provide a **technical toolbox** from which teams should select approaches appropriate to their game.

The following are examples rather than mandatory features.

| **Area** | **Examples** |
|:-|:-|
| **Data & State** | ScriptableObjects, configuration data, shared state |
| **Communication** | Delegates, events, `Action`, `Func`, ScriptableObject event channels |
| **Architecture** | Interfaces, composition, appropriate design patterns |
| **Movement** | Transform, Rigidbody, CharacterController, NavMesh |
| **Scene Architecture** | Scene management, additive scene loading, persistent systems |
| **Animation & Feel** | Animator, interpolation, easing, `AnimationCurve`, tweening |
| **Game Flow** | Events, coroutines, conditions, orchestration |
| **Behaviour** | Finite State Machines, State pattern, predicates |
| **Presentation** | Camera, audio, animation, feedback, lighting |
| **Rendering** | Lighting, LOD, culling, materials |

Teams should be able to explain **why** significant technical approaches were selected and what problem each approach solves.

A simpler solution that is appropriate, robust and well implemented is preferable to unnecessary complexity.

Technical ambition can contribute to excellent work where a team successfully tackles a challenging problem. **Complexity alone does not attract marks.**


# Development Process

The project should remain **playable throughout development**.

Teams should work towards a complete small experience early and then progressively improve it rather than developing a large collection of independent features and attempting to integrate them near the deadline.

Indicative technical checkpoints will take place during scheduled 3D Game Engine Development classes.


## Checkpoint 1 - First Playable

The project should demonstrate the beginnings of its core gameplay:

- player movement;
- primary interaction/mechanic;
- basic environment;
- initial project architecture.


## Checkpoint 2 - Complete Gameplay Loop

The complete approximately **2-3 minute experience should be playable from beginning to end**, even if presentation and implementation remain basic.

The emphasis should now begin to move from adding scope towards improving what already exists.


## Checkpoint 3 - Technical Integration

Major game systems should be integrated.

The team should be able to demonstrate and explain significant architectural decisions including communication between systems, data/state management and relevant design techniques.


## Checkpoint 4 - Refinement

The team should demonstrate evidence of:

- testing;
- debugging;
- technical refinement;
- improved game feel;
- architectural improvement where appropriate;
- lighting and presentation improvements;
- removal or simplification of unnecessary features.


## Final - Polished Playable Demo

The final submission should represent the **best version of the same small playable experience**, rather than a substantially larger game.

Assessment is based on the submitted Unity project together with evidence provided through the:

All three are **mandatory components of the GCA**.

## GitHub Repository - MANDATORY

The complete Unity project must be developed using the team's **GitHub repository throughout the assessment**.

The repository must:

- contain the complete Unity project source;
- demonstrate meaningful development history;
- contain regular, meaningful commits;
- include an appropriate `.gitignore`;
- clearly separate project-owned and third-party assets;
- contain a README;
- remain accessible to the lecturer.

GitHub history may be examined when assessing **Iteration & Improvement**, verifying nominated evidence, and establishing development history and authorship.

Repository quality is **not assessed by counting commits**. A small number of meaningful commits is more useful than a large number of trivial commits.

A repository created or populated primarily near the submission deadline does not provide convincing evidence of incremental development.

> :warning: **If the GitHub repository is missing or inaccessible, the project cannot be fully assessed.**

## Playable Build - MANDATORY

A working build of the final demo must be provided.

The build must:

- start correctly;
- contain the complete assessed approximately **2-3 minute experience**;
- require no development tools to play;
- provide sufficient information for the player to understand the controls.

The 2-3 minute duration is an **indicative scope target rather than a strict stopwatch limit**. Minor variation caused by player behaviour or the nature of the game will not be penalised.

However, substantially increasing the length, number of levels or quantity of content does not attract additional marks.

> :warning: **Always test the submitted build on a machine other than the primary development machine before submission.**


# Structured Group Screencast - MANDATORY

Submit **one structured group screencast** demonstrating the team's achievement against the assessment criteria.

All team members must participate.

The screencast should combine demonstration of the running game with inspection and explanation of relevant Unity scenes, components, source code and GitHub history.

The screencast should follow this structure:

### 1. Complete Playthrough

Demonstrate the complete approximately **2-3 minute playable experience** from beginning to end.

### 2. Gameplay Systems & Integration

Demonstrate and explain the team's **two nominated gameplay systems** and how they integrate with the wider game.

### 3. C# & Software Design

Demonstrate the three nominated examples covering:

- Data & State;
- System Communication;
- Software Design.

For each example, explain the:

**Problem → Solution → Benefit**

### 4. Technical Craft

Demonstrate the team's **two nominated examples of technical craft** and explain how they were deliberately refined.

### 5. Iteration & Improvement

Demonstrate the team's **two nominated before-and-after improvements**.

Use GitHub history where appropriate to show how each implementation changed.

Explain:

**Initial Implementation → Identified Problem → Change → Resulting Improvement**

### 6. Technical Finish & Performance

Show that the final demo is stable and complete.

Briefly identify any significant technical finishing work, including any technical or performance problem you found and how you resolved it.

The screencast is **assessment evidence rather than a video-production exercise**. Marks are awarded for the technical work demonstrated and explained, not for sophisticated video editing or production.

> :warning: **If the structured group screencast is missing or inaccessible, the project cannot be fully assessed.**


# Week 12 Live Technical Demonstration - MANDATORY

Each group must give a **live demonstration of its final project to the 3D Game Engine Development lecturer during Week 12**.

This is a mandatory **3D Game Engine Development assessment activity** and is **separate from any presentation, demonstration or assessment undertaken for the Universal Design Project (UDP) module**.

During the demonstration, the group should be prepared to:

- run the complete playable demo;
- demonstrate nominated technical evidence;
- open relevant Unity scenes and components;
- locate and explain relevant C# source code;
- demonstrate relevant GitHub history;
- explain significant technical decisions;
- demonstrate before-and-after improvements where requested;
- discuss problems encountered and how they were addressed;
- answer questions about the implementation.

The lecturer may select any part of the submitted project for further examination.

Students may be asked to locate code, explain its behaviour, identify dependencies, discuss alternative approaches, or demonstrate how a system developed during the project.

The screencast provides the team's **structured presentation of its strongest evidence**.

The Week 12 demonstration provides an opportunity for **live lecturer-directed examination and verification**.

> :warning: **The Week 12 technical demonstration is mandatory and cannot be replaced by the UDP demonstration.**
